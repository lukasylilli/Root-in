# Lernplanung

## Why plan at all?

Learning a language fails for one reason more than any other: it depends on mood. A routine removes the mood from the equation. When studying becomes a duty rather than a decision, you stop negotiating with yourself every morning — and you keep moving even on the days you don't feel like it.

A plan also makes progress visible. After three months you can look back and see exactly what you did.

## The seven skills

Every plan on this page is built from the same seven skills. Learn the German names — you will need them anyway.

| German | English |
|---|---|
| Grammatik | Grammar |
| Wortschatz | Vocabulary |
| Auswendiglernen | Memorization |
| Lesen | Reading |
| Schreiben | Writing |
| Sprechen | Speaking |
| Hören | Listening |

**Auswendiglernen** covers the material you simply have to know by heart: Redemittel, Nomen-Verb-Verbindungen, Nomen-Adjektiv-Verbindungen, Verben mit Präpositionen, unregelmäßige Verben.

### Classes and video lessons

If you attend a course, the hours you spend in class count as part of your plan.

If you study on your own, you can learn the same material from our YouTube channel — the address is on the **Lernquellen** page.

Either way, that time counts as **Grammatik** only.

## The daily base layer

Two short tasks that run **every single day**, no matter which model you choose:

- **10 minutes Karteikarten** — flashcard review with the Leitner system, in a separate app
- **10 minutes Hören** — listening to training audio, listen and repeat

On a day where you work only half the day, stretch both to **30 minutes**.

Two things to keep in mind:

1. The base layer sits **outside** your study plan and outside the 25-minute rule. It is review, not new learning.
2. It pauses only on your weekly rest day.

Note that Wortschatz and Hören therefore appear twice — once in the base layer as daily contact, once inside the plan as serious study. They are not duplicates:

| | Base layer | Plan block |
|---|---|---|
| **Wortschatz** | Leitner flashcard review | New words from your book / from Lernquellen |
| **Hören** | Training audio, listen and repeat | Book audio: listen until you know it, write it down, compare with the transcript |

**The base layer keeps the language alive. The plan blocks move it forward.**

## Which model is for me?

- **Wochenplan** — your days are similar and you want something simple. One focus per day.
- **Tagesplan** — you have about 3.5 free hours every day and want the fastest possible progress.
- **Schichtplan** — your working hours change from day to day (shift work, irregular jobs, students with uneven timetables).

## Model 1 — Wochenplan

Each day of the week gets one focus. Write it out from Saturday to Friday, including the rest day.

Example:

| Day | Focus |
|---|---|
| Saturday | Lehrbuch |
| Sunday | Lehrbuch |
| Monday | Arbeitsbuch |
| Tuesday | Wortschatz |
| Wednesday | Hören + Schreiben |
| Thursday | Sprechen |
| Friday | Rest day |

Simple to follow, and easy to see at a glance whether you kept it.

## Model 2 — Tagesplan

The same plan every day. Requires roughly **3.5 free hours**.

You work through all seven skills, one block each:

**7 blocks × (25 minutes study + 5 minutes break) = 3.5 hours**

| Block | Skill |
|---|---|
| 1 | Grammatik |
| 2 | Wortschatz |
| 3 | Auswendiglernen |
| 4 | Lesen |
| 5 | Schreiben |
| 6 | Sprechen |
| 7 | Hören |

If you have more than 3.5 hours available, you have three options for the extra time:

- divide it evenly among all seven skills,
- put all of it into the skill you are weakest in, or
- use it to review what you did the day before.

## Model 3 — Schichtplan

For anyone whose working hours are not fixed — for example a nurse who has some days completely free, some night shifts, some mornings, and some full days at work.

Sort your days into three types:

| Day type | Karteikarten | Hören (base) | Plan blocks |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** — completely free | 10 min | 10 min | several 25+5 blocks |
| **B** — at work all day | 10 min | 10 min | — |
| **C** — half day at work | 30 min | 30 min | — |
| **Rest day** | — | — | — |

The base layer stretches to fit the day you have. On a free day it goes back to 10 minutes, because the plan blocks already cover Wortschatz and Hören properly.

**Important:** on a free day you do **not** do all seven skills. Instead, decide in advance how often each skill appears per week — for example Grammatik once a week, Sprechen once a week. Then spread them across your free days.

Example: if Monday and Thursday are your free days, do Grammatik on Monday and skip it on Thursday, because Grammatik is done for that week. Thursday becomes Schreiben instead.

Your free days are also where the textbooks belong: **Lehrbuch / Kursbuch** and **Arbeitsbuch**. Every German course system is built on these two books, and the Arbeitsbuch exercises always follow the Lehrbuch lesson.

How many blocks you fit into a free day is up to you. Only the shared rules below must hold.

## Shared rules

These apply to all three models:

1. **25 minutes study, 5 minutes break.** Never study a skill for less than 25 minutes in a plan block.
2. **One full rest day per week.** No studying at all, not even the base layer.
3. **Divide your time fairly** across the seven skills. None of them may be neglected.
4. **Every skill at least once a week.**
5. **The base layer runs every day** except the rest day.
6. **Sources for each skill** are listed on the Lernquellen page.

## Make it your own

Your plan does not have to contain only German. You can add anything else and give it a fixed time:

- 06:00 — sport
- 07:20 — 10 minutes Karteikarten on the metro
- 21:00 — Hören while cooking

The more specific the time, the more likely it happens.

## Your streak

If you skip a day without a good reason — illness and similar count as good reasons — your streak resets to zero.

Resetting is normal. Not coming back is not. And note that the reset applies to your day count only, never to your material: if you finished lesson 3, you continue with lesson 4.
